    St Mary the Virgin, Kington

    Kington

    A regular act of worship every Sunday at 10:00am — normally a Common Worship Order One Service of Holy Communion. We're fortunate to have the services of our organist and a talented choir; the aim behind all our worship is to offer the best we can in thanksgiving, welcoming and engaging while retaining a real sense of awe.

    Sundays, 10:00am — Holy Communion

    St Peter's, Titley

    Titley

    A welcoming balance of the traditional and contemporary. Early morning Communion follows Common Worship Order Two, in the language used by Shakespeare and loved by so many; on the third Sunday of the month we use Common Worship Order One. Afterwards, we're normally welcomed by Tony and June at "The Old Priory" next to the church for coffee and fellowship.

    "To create a place of worship that is relevant to life in 21st century England — even somewhere as quiet as Titley."

    Third Sunday — Common Worship Order One

    St Stephen's, Old Radnor

    Old Radnor

    Worship follows Common Worship Order One for Communion Services. We're privileged to possess an extremely fine organ, with the oldest free-standing organ case in the UK. Ruth, our talented organist (shared with Kinnerton), leads the singing. On the third Sunday, our evening worship — "Junior Praise" — is less formal and designed to be relevant to the young and young at heart.

    Third Sunday evening — Junior Praise
    Home of the UK's oldest free-standing organ case

    St Mary's, Kinnerton

    Kinnerton

    A very small church down in the Radnor Valley, surrounded by hills, firmly over the border. Ring the bell to call our handful of worshippers to church, and you can hear the ewes and lambs just over the churchyard wall — handy for praying for the farm when you can hear it going on all around you. We are small, but there is life here.

    Kinnerton doesn't have its own Parish Giving Scheme page — donations here are given via St Stephen's, Old Radnor.

    St Thomas à Becket, Huntington

    Huntington

    A Grade II* listed building with ancient oak pews and stained glass by the acclaimed Henry Payne. Its organ was originally built by the Positive Organ Company of London, founded by Thomas Casson — a banker turned organ builder whose ideas were advanced for the time — and has recently undergone a substantial upgrade. Huntington is a predominantly farming community, three miles west of Kington on the Welsh border, first recorded in the Domesday Book of 1086.

    "We live in a peaceful part of the world, but entering the church takes you to a quieter place still."

    Holy Eucharist, every 2nd Sunday — Evensong, every 4th Sunday
    Silence & prayer, alternate Wednesdays
    Grade II* listed
