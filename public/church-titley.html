    St Peter's, Titley

    Traditional language, a warm welcome, and coffee afterwards at The Old Priory.

    A welcoming balance of the traditional and contemporary. Early morning Communion follows Common Worship Order Two, in the language used by Shakespeare and loved by so many; on the third Sunday of the month we use Common Worship Order One. Afterwards, we're normally welcomed by Tony and June at "The Old Priory" next to the church for coffee and fellowship.

    "To create a place of worship that is relevant to life in 21st century England — even somewhere as quiet as Titley."

    History & architecture

    The site has been a place of worship since around 1120, when it was home to a small priory — a cell of the Tironensian Order, one of only four such houses in England. It is thought that the present church was rebuilt in the 1860s, incorporating a west tower built around 1834 and designed and paid for by Lady Elizabeth Coffin Greenly of Titley Court; she and her mother are buried beneath it. St Peter's is listed Grade II (Historic England list entry 1081465, first listed 17 December 1986).

    Monuments

    The churchyard holds the grave of General Lázár Mészáros, a Hungarian national hero who served as Supreme Commander of the Army and Minister of National Defence in Hungary's 1849 revolutionary government. He died at Titley and was buried here in November 1858, and a memorial plaque was unveiled by the Hungarian Embassy in November 2019. It is thought that his remains were later exhumed and reburied at Baja Cathedral in Hungary in 1991.
