St Mary's, Kinnerton

A small church in the Radnor Valley — small in number, but full of life.

A very small church down in the Radnor Valley, surrounded by hills, firmly over the border. Ring the bell to call our handful of worshippers to church, and you can hear the ewes and lambs just over the churchyard wall — handy for praying for the farm when you can hear it going on all around you. We are small, but there is life here.

History & architecture

The church of St Mary is located in the centre of the village of Kinnerton, in a raised churchyard used as a cemetery. It was built in 1884–5 in lancet style to designs of Thomas Nicholson. Although the church is an entirely nineteenth-century construction, it replaced a small, earlier church that had been enlarged and refitted in 1833–4 by Burgoyne.

The church is constructed of rock-faced masonry with lancets in pinkish ashlar, including the east triplet, and slate roofs. It consists of a nave and narrower chancel under the same steeply-pitched roofline, a west bellcote, a gabled south porch with an open cusped timber front, and a north-east vestry under a catslide roof; a chimney projects from the north side of the chancel roof.

The interior is lofty, with scissor-truss roofs except for the wagon ceiling above the sanctuary. The chancel arch is of wood on wall-posts with stiff-leaf capitals. Fittings include a font with a round bowl and attached shafts (1885), and an elaborate carved oak pulpit by Nicholson. The stained glass in the south chancel is by Camm & Co (1940).
