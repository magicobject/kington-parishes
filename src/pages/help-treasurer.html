Treasurer and Finance

St Mary’s, Kington is run by its Parochial Church Council (PCC). The PCC is a registered charity, and its members are the trustees. The treasurer looks after the PCC’s money: keeping the records, paying the bills, reporting to the PCC and producing the annual accounts.

You don’t need to be an accountant. The treasurer has the help of a part-time bookkeeper and an Independent Examiner, and there are clear routines and templates for almost everything. What matters most is being organised, careful and willing to ask questions.

If you’d like to know more, or might be willing to help, please contact the Parish Office.

Understanding church funds

Every pound the church holds belongs to one of four kinds of fund. The kind of fund decides what the money may be spent on.

Type of fundWho decides how it is spentTypical examples
General (unrestricted)The PCC, for any church purposeCollections, regular giving, most legacies
DesignatedThe PCC sets money aside for a purpose, and can release it laterA fund for a planned project
RestrictedThe donor: it must be spent on the purpose they gave it forAn appeal for the organ, a grant for repairs, a legacy “for the fabric of the church”
EndowmentThe donor: the capital is kept and only the income is spentAn old bequest invested to provide a yearly income

What the donor said is what decides the fund. It might be in a will, a grant offer or an appeal leaflet. Honouring those wishes is one of the PCC’s most important duties, and the treasurer’s job is to record any restriction when the money arrives and see that it is respected.

Where the money comes from

  • Regular giving. Our biggest source of income is the Parish Giving Scheme, which collects regular donations by direct debit and claims Gift Aid automatically. Standing orders and weekly envelopes also contribute.
  • Collections and donations. Gifts at services, in the donation box, by contactless card reader or QR code, and by Charities Aid Foundation vouchers.
  • Gift Aid. Tax reclaimed from HMRC on gifts from UK taxpayers who have signed a declaration. It adds 25p to every £1 given.
  • Fees. Weddings and funerals. Part of each fee goes to the Diocese.
  • Parish Hall hire. From regular groups and one-off bookings.
  • The Parish News. Advertising and sales.
  • Fundraising. The fete, the community shop, concerts, the Bonus Ball and other events.
  • Legacies and grants. These are often given for a particular purpose.
  • VAT refunds. The Listed Places of Worship scheme refunds VAT on repairs to our listed church.

If you would like to support St Mary’s, please see our giving page or contact the Parish Office.

Where the money goes

  • The Parish Offer. Our contribution to the Diocese of Hereford towards the cost of clergy and ministry. It is the largest single payment each year.
  • Running the church and hall. Insurance, electricity, water, cleaning, repairs and maintenance.
  • Worship and music. The organist and choir, music, service sheets, flowers and live-streaming.
  • Clergy and office costs. Expenses for our clergy, and the benefice office, shared with the other parishes.
  • Mission and charity. Special collections and donations passed on to the charities they were given for.
  • Building projects. Major work, such as the recent heating and organ projects, is usually funded by appeals, grants and legacies given for that purpose.

The annual accounts

The church’s financial year runs from 1 January to 31 December. Early each year the treasurer:

  1. Checks the accounts against the year-end bank and investment statements.
  2. Records what each fund received, spent and has left.
  3. Writes a short financial report for the Annual Report.
  4. Sends everything to the Independent Examiner.
  5. Presents the examined accounts to the PCC and then to the Annual Parochial Church Meeting.

The accounts are then filed with the Charity Commission, where anyone can read them.

How we keep the money safe

  • Every payment is backed by an invoice or receipt and recorded in the accounts.
  • Bank records are checked regularly against the accounts.
  • Restricted money is tracked so that it is only spent on its stated purpose.
  • Personal information, such as Gift Aid declarations and donor records, is kept securely and seen only by those who need it, in line with data-protection law.
  • The accounts are examined independently every year.

Shared Dropbox. St Mary’s PCC keeps its records in a shared Dropbox containing two folders, PCC Accounts and PCC Business. The Dropbox is currently controlled by David Redmayne.

Could you help?

The treasurer role needs:

  • a few hours a week, more around year-end
  • comfort with spreadsheets and online banking
  • care with detail and confidentiality
  • willingness to report clearly to the PCC

Training and guidance are available from the Diocese of Hereford, and there is a full handover for anyone taking on the role. We also welcome help with smaller tasks, such as counting collections, running fundraising events or helping with Gift Aid.

Please contact the Parish Office for an informal conversation.
