When something goes wrong

A change didn't look right, or you're not sure what just happened — here's what to do. Short version: it's very hard to break anything permanently, and putting it right is always just a conversation with Claude, not a technical problem for you to solve.

Your options

Three ways to put it right

None of these need any technical know-how — just tell Claude what's wrong, in your own words, the same way you asked for the change in the first place.

1. Ask Claude

This is always the first step. Say what looks wrong — "that's not what I meant", "the date's wrong", "it looks odd on my phone" — and Claude will look at what it just changed and work out what to do next.

2. Revert the change

Because Git keeps a complete history (see Git & GitHub, in plain English), any change can be fully undone — just ask Claude to "revert" or "undo" it, and the page goes back to exactly how it looked before.

3. Or just fail forward

Often quicker than undoing: instead of reverting, simply describe the fix you actually want — "make the heading bigger, not smaller" — and Claude edits again from where things stand. Whichever of the two feels simpler for the situation is the right one to ask for.

Worth knowing

A few reassurances

Nothing is permanently broken

Every version of every page is saved in Git's history, forever — there's always a way back to how things looked before, no matter what went wrong.

Most mistakes are caught before they go live

Claude runs the site's automatic tests and its accessibility and security checks before anything reaches the live site (see Making a change) — so most problems never make it as far as a visitor seeing them.

Not seeing your change yet?

It might just not have gone live yet — check /updates for your change at the top of the list before assuming something's wrong.

Still stuck?

Get in touch at web@kingtonparishes.org.uk and whoever currently looks after the website will help sort it out.
