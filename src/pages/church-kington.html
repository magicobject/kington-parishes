St Mary the Virgin, Kington

Sunday worship, choir, and a livestreamed service each week, near the heart of the town.

A regular act of worship every Sunday at 10:00am — normally a Common Worship Order One Service of Holy Communion. We're fortunate to have the services of our organist and a talented choir; the aim behind all our worship is to offer the best we can in thanksgiving, welcoming and engaging while retaining a real sense of awe.

History & architecture

The church — properly the Church of St Mary the Virgin, though "St Mary's" is the name everyone uses day to day — stands on rising ground above the town, close to the site of the former Kington Castle, founded in the reign of Henry I (1100–1135). A defensive tower was built here around 1200, originally freestanding; the nave and aisles followed around 1300, the chancel was rebuilt and a south porch added in the early 14th century, and a Decorated-style south chapel was added around 1320. The double-broach spire was rebuilt in 1794, and an outer north aisle was added around 1874.

Inside, a Norman circular font with rope moulding and an incised zig-zag pattern survives from the church's earliest phase. The church is listed Grade I (Historic England list entry 1208031, first listed 8 October 1953).

Monuments

The church holds the alabaster tomb-chest of Sir Thomas Vaughan of Hergest Court (d. 1469, killed at the Battle of Edgecote fighting for the Yorkists), and his wife Ellen Gethin, its effigies flanked by ten carved alabaster angels holding shields; the tomb was restored in 1845. A memorial tablet to William Matthews (d. 1688) also survives. It is thought that Lady Margaret Hawkins — of the Vaughan family, and wife of the Elizabethan naval commander Sir John Hawkins — endowed the church, with her bequest said to have required pupils of the adjoining Lady Hawkins School to attend Sunday services.

Bells

St Mary's has a ring of six bells: five cast by Rudhall of Gloucester between 1736 and 1739, and one by William Evans of Chepstow in 1764. The ring was refurbished in 1978.

Organ

It is thought that the organ was installed in 1883 by J.W. Walker & Sons, and rebuilt in 1959 by Henry Willis & Sons.

Lychgate

The churchyard's lychgate — 18th-century, carved stone, with a domed roof — is separately listed Grade II*.
