# Kington Parishes Advent Calendar: Content Plan (25 doors)

Working title: **"The Long Thread of Light"**

## 1. The arc, in a few lines

The calendar tells one story: God has been leaning toward the world since the beginning. Each door adds one promise, one waiting person or one step closer to Bethlehem.

- **Days 1-8: Promise and presence.** Creation, the fall and its first promise, Noah's rainbow, Abraham's stars, Jacob's ladder, the burning bush, Passover and the sea, then the cloud of glory that travels with the people. By Day 8 the idea of God *with* his people is planted.
- **Days 9-13: A king, and a small town.** Ruth and Bethlehem, David the shepherd king, then Isaiah's light and shoot from Jesse's stump, and Micah naming Bethlehem.
- **Days 14-15: Exile and waiting.** The long dark of exile, then comfort and the quiet years.
- **Days 16-19: The first announcements.** Zechariah, Mary, Joseph and Elizabeth.
- **Days 20-24: The road.** Journey, far-off seekers, no room, shepherds, angels.
- **Day 25: Christmas Day.** The birth, with the "Word became flesh" as the landing.

**Magi.** They are *foreshadowed* on Day 21 (travelling from far away, drawn by a star, with Isaiah 60). Day 21 says plainly that they arrive later. The full visit is a bonus door for 6 January.

**Simeon and Anna.** They belong after the birth (Candlemas, 2 Feb), so they are bonus doors. Their spirit of patient waiting is carried by Zechariah, Elizabeth and Day 15.

**Why not more Old Testament?** The brief asks for reach back and forward. These 15 Old Testament doors are chosen because each is later echoed in the Christmas stories (light, stars, a ladder, God dwelling with us, Bethlehem, a shepherd king, a shoot from a stump). The fall is deliberately short and gentle.

## 2. Tone and voice guide

1. **Read-aloud first.** Short sentences. Concrete things (bread, dust, stars, sheep) before abstractions. Every text should work spoken in a candlelit church for a 7-year-old and a 90-year-old.
2. **Warm, never saccharine.** Name real hardship (exile, fear, a long journey) honestly, then let hope arrive.
3. **Invite, don't insist.** Wonder questions are open ("I wonder..."). No hard sell, no guilt, no "you must believe".
4. **Bible-faithful, lightly told.** Stay close to the text. Where a detail is imagined (a feeling, a smell), keep it modest and never contradict Scripture. Mark nothing as fact that the Bible doesn't say.
5. **Inclusive welcome.** Speak to those who come on a Sunday, those who come at Christmas, and those who are searching. Prayers use "we" and plain words.

## 3. The recurring motif

- **The golden thread.** One continuous gold line runs through all 25 pictures. It starts as a spark of light on Day 1 and ends wrapped around the manger on Day 25. It can bend into a rainbow edge, a ladder rail, a river, a cloud edge, a road or a star-tail.
- **Small star in every picture.** One tiny four-point star is always hidden somewhere, plus the day's own hidden detail (below). Visitors learn that the thread and star are "always there".
- **Technical note.** On the website the thread could be a single `<path>` that is drawn progressively across the days opened, so the calendar page itself slowly shows the thread growing (with a reduced-motion fallback).
- **Palette.** Midnight blue, deep teal, warm cream, one accent per day, and gold (#C9A227 or similar) for the thread. Check all text/background pairs for WCAG AA before use.
- **Style.** Flat vector, thick rounded lines, minimal detail, no faces drawn in detail (simple dots or none) so figures are inclusive and read at small sizes.

## 4. The 25 doors

### Day 1: "Let There Be Light"
**Bible:** Genesis 1:1-5 (KJV quote: "Let there be light")
**Story:** Before there were hills or rivers or people, there was only dark and quiet. And God spoke. "Let there be light." And light came, soft at first, the way morning creeps over a ridge. God looked at it and saw that it was good. Then God kept going: sky, sea, earth, trees, birds, creatures of every kind, and last of all, people, to share it with. The whole story begins with love making room for others. Every night since then, every dawn has been a small reminder that the dark does not get the last word. This calendar follows one thread of that light, from the very first morning all the way to a baby in a manger.
**Wonder:** I wonder what the very first light looked like?
**Activity:** Sit in a dark room, light one candle, and notice how much a tiny flame changes.
**Prayer:** God of the first light, thank you for the world you made. Help us to see it and love it. Amen.
**Illustration:** Dark midnight-blue circle; a single gold spark at centre unrolls into a golden thread that curls out like a sunrise. Faint outlines of hill, tree and bird emerging. Mood: hushed, expectant, warm.
**Alt-text:** A golden spark of light begins to unroll against a deep blue night, with hills and birds just appearing.
**Hidden detail:** The tiny star (introduced here), plus a very small owl.
**Local link (optional):** The Hergest Ridge or Radnor Forest skyline at dawn. Only as a general image; no specific claim. *Needs confirmation by the parish if a specific photograph is used.*

### Day 2: "The Whisper in the Garden"
**Bible:** Genesis 3:8-15, 21 (WEB paraphrase; no direct quote needed)
**Story:** In the garden, God and the first people walked and talked in the cool of the evening. But they chose their own way, and something broke: shame, hiding, and the first hurt between people and God. God did not leave them. He came looking, "Where are you?" Even then, he made clothes for them and spoke a promise that someday one of their family would put things right. It is a quiet promise, almost a whisper, but the whole Bible leans towards it. We all know what it is to get things wrong and want to hide. This door says God comes looking, with a promise, not with a slammed door.
**Wonder:** I wonder how it felt to hear God ask, "Where are you?"
**Activity:** Play gentle hide-and-seek, and when someone is found, say "I'm glad I found you."
**Prayer:** God who comes looking for us, find us when we hide, and help us begin again. Amen.
**Illustration:** A leafy tree with a half-hidden figure behind it; a gold thread trails from the tree towards a distant, tiny, bright horizon. Warm green and gold, dusk sky.
**Alt-text:** Two small figures half-hidden by a garden tree at dusk while a golden thread leads to a distant light.
**Hidden detail:** A tiny snake pattern shaped like the letter S hiding in the bark.
**Local link:** None needed.
**Review note:** Keep "fall" language gentle and non-judgemental; avoid blaming Eve.

### Day 3: "A Bow in the Clouds"
**Bible:** Genesis 8:20-9:17 (KJV: "I do set my bow in the cloud" Gen 9:13)
**Story:** The rain had come for so long that the world was nothing but water. Noah and his family, and all the animals, were safe in the big boat. At last the water went down, and the dry land appeared. They stepped out, and God made them a promise: never again. And as a sign God said, "I do set my bow in the cloud" (KJV). A rainbow. Not a soldier's bow, a hanging-up bow, pointed away from us, a reminder that God keeps promises. Next time you see a rainbow after rain, whether over the Radnor hills or a puddle in the road, you can say that this is God's promise, still there.
**Wonder:** I wonder what Noah thought the first time he saw the rainbow?
**Activity:** Colour a rainbow, or hang a prism in a window to make one on the wall.
**Prayer:** Faithful God, you keep your promises. Help us to trust you when the storms come. Amen.
**Illustration:** A small boat on calm water; a bright arc of colours (muted, soft) above; the gold thread runs along the arc's edge like a gold lining. Animals' silhouettes on the shore.
**Alt-text:** A small boat on calm water beneath a soft rainbow edged with a thread of gold.
**Hidden detail:** A dove carrying a leaf (very small).
**Local link:** A Herefordshire rainbow after a shower: suggest visitors send photos? *Needs confirmation by the parish if images are collected (permission and safeguarding).*

### Day 4: "Count the Stars"
**Bible:** Genesis 12:1-3; 15:1-6 (WEB: "Look now toward the sky, and count the stars" Gen 15:5)
**Story:** Abram was an old man with no children when God told him to leave his country and go to a land that God would show him, and promised that all the families of the earth would be blessed through him. So Abram packed up, left his home, and went, without a map. One night God took him outside and said, "Look now toward the sky, and count the stars, if you are able to count them." And God said, "So will your offspring be." Abram looked at the huge, glittering sky and believed. He couldn't see how it would happen. But he trusted the one who promised. Many hundreds of years later a child would be born from that long line, and the blessing was meant for everyone, not just one family.
**Wonder:** I wonder what it's like to trust someone when you can't see the road?
**Activity:** Go out on a clear night (or look at a star map) and try to count. Or cut paper stars.
**Prayer:** God of the stars, help us to trust you even when we cannot see the way. Amen.
**Illustration:** Tall figure with a staff beneath a sky packed with stars; the gold thread loops from the figure's hand up through the stars. Tents in the distance. Indigo and gold.
**Alt-text:** A traveller with a staff gazes at a sky full of stars joined by a golden thread.
**Hidden detail:** One star is larger than the rest.
**Local link:** Dark-sky night over the hills. *Needs confirmation by the parish* before claiming any "dark sky" status or specific stargazing sites.

### Day 5: "A Ladder to Heaven"
**Bible:** Genesis 28:10-17 (WEB: "Surely Yahweh is in this place" v.16)
**Story:** Jacob was running away, tired, lonely and in trouble of his own making. With a stone for a pillow he fell asleep in the open. And he dreamed: a stairway reaching from earth up to heaven, with angels going up and down. And God spoke, promising to be with him and never to leave him. Jacob woke up amazed. "Surely Yahweh is in this place, and I didn't know it." He had thought he was alone in the middle of nowhere. It turned out to be the very spot where heaven touched earth. Nobody is so far from home that God cannot find them. And one day heaven would come right down the ladder.
**Wonder:** I wonder where you have felt close to God without expecting to?
**Activity:** Build a ladder from paper strips; on each rung write something you are thankful for.
**Prayer:** God who is with us everywhere, help us notice you in unexpected places. Amen.
**Illustration:** A figure asleep on hillside, head on a stone; a golden ladder (the thread!) rises into a cloudy sky with small winged shapes. Night-blue, soft gold.
**Alt-text:** A sleeping traveller on a hillside dreams of a golden ladder reaching to the sky.
**Hidden detail:** A tiny bird on the stone.
**Local link:** "Thin places" / hilltop churches. A natural fit for Old Radnor's hilltop setting. *Needs confirmation by the parish* before any specific claims about the church or its history.

### Day 6: "A Bush That Would Not Burn Up"
**Bible:** Exodus 3:1-14 (WEB: "I AM THAT I AM" v.14, KJV wording acceptable)
**Story:** Moses was minding sheep in the desert, a long way from the palace he grew up in. Then he saw something odd: a bush on fire that wasn't burning away. He went closer, and a voice said, "Take off your sandals, for the place you are standing on is holy ground." God said that he had seen how his people suffered in Egypt, had heard their cry, and was sending Moses. Moses made excuses, asking who he was to go to Pharaoh. God said, "Certainly I will be with you." That was the whole answer. God does not stay up in the sky; God hears, comes close, and sends people to help.
**Wonder:** I wonder why God chose a plain bush and a shepherd?
**Activity:** Take off your shoes for a minute and stand still in the grass or on the carpet, noticing the ground.
**Prayer:** God who hears our cries, make us listeners too, and show us how to help. Amen.
**Illustration:** A bush with leaves outlined in gold flame (leaves still green), sandals left on ground, a small flock of sheep. Warm orange/gold sky. The thread forms the flame.
**Alt-text:** A green bush glows with golden flames that do not burn it, with a pair of sandals in the foreground.
**Hidden detail:** A tiny sandal-print in the sand.
**Local link:** None needed.

### Day 7: "Scarlet on the Doorframe"
**Bible:** Exodus 12:1-14, 34, 14:21-31 (told briefly)
**Story:** Long ago the people were slaves in Egypt, and the night of their rescue came. God told them to prepare a special meal, eaten in a hurry, ready to travel, and to mark their doorframes so that the family inside would be safe. That night they left in such haste that they carried their dough, still unrisen, in kneading troughs wrapped up in their clothes. At the edge of the sea the army chased them, and the waters parted. They walked through on dry ground. Ever since, God's people have told this story every year: *we were rescued.* It is the great pattern: God sets people free. When Jesus comes, he will be called a rescuer too.
**Wonder:** I wonder what it felt like to walk through the sea?
**Activity:** Bake or share some flat bread. Eat it quickly, "ready to travel".
**Prayer:** God who sets us free, thank you for rescuing your people. Free us from what holds us back. Amen.
**Illustration:** A doorway with a scarlet line on the frame; behind it, a wall of blue water with a path through it. The gold thread weaves between the water walls like a path. Red, blue, gold.
**Alt-text:** A doorframe marked in red opens onto a path of dry ground between two walls of blue water.
**Hidden detail:** A tiny lamb in the lower corner.
**Local link:** None needed.
**Review note:** Passover is Jewish heritage; phrase as "God's people" with respect and avoid supersessionist wording ("replaced"). Do not describe the last plague in detail, which is unsuitable for young children.

### Day 8: "A Cloud by Day, a Fire by Night"
**Bible:** Exodus 13:21-22; 40:34-38
**Story:** The people were free, but the desert was wide and frightening. How would they find the way? God gave them a sign: a tall cloud by day and a pillar of fire by night, moving ahead of them. When it moved, they moved. When it rested, they rested. And when they built a special tent to worship in, the glory of God filled it, and the cloud settled over it. God was not far off; God lived among them, travelling with them in their tents. Hold on to that picture, because it is exactly what will happen at Bethlehem: God, camped among us.
**Wonder:** I wonder what it's like to have God so close you can see the cloud?
**Activity:** Make a cloud with cotton wool glued on blue paper and add a gold thread that leads on.
**Prayer:** God who travels with us, guide our steps and stay close when we're afraid. Amen.
**Illustration:** A line of small tents on a wide desert; a tall soft cloud above with the thread running through as a pillar of gold; stars at the edge. Sand, indigo, gold.
**Alt-text:** A row of tents in the desert beneath a tall cloud lit with a golden thread that guides the way.
**Hidden detail:** A tiny tent with a lit lamp inside.
**Local link:** None needed.

### Day 9: "Ruth Stays"
**Bible:** Ruth 1:1-18; 4:13-17
**Story:** Ruth was a young woman from a foreign land who lost her husband. Her mother-in-law Naomi, also a widow, decided to go home to Bethlehem. Naomi told her to go back to her own family. But Ruth held on: "where you go, I will go; and where you stay, I will stay. Your people will be my people, and your God my God." Together they came to Bethlehem at harvest time. Ruth gathered leftover grain in the fields, kindness found her through a generous farmer called Boaz, and in time they married. Their great-grandson was David, the shepherd king. So the family of Jesus holds a foreigner who chose loyalty. God's story is wider than we think.
**Wonder:** I wonder what made Ruth so brave and so loyal?
**Activity:** Do one loyal act for someone, such as a card or a visit to someone on their own.
**Prayer:** God of faithful friends, bless those who stay beside others. Make us loyal and kind. Amen.
**Illustration:** Two women walking on a path through golden wheat towards a small hill town; the gold thread is the path and the wheat heads. Gold, wheat-yellow, blue sky.
**Alt-text:** Two women walk through golden wheat fields towards the small hill town of Bethlehem.
**Hidden detail:** A single sheaf tied with thread.
**Local link:** Harvest in Herefordshire fields; harvest festival traditions. *Needs confirmation by the parish* before naming any specific local farm or event.

### Day 10: "The Shepherd Boy Chosen"
**Bible:** 1 Samuel 16:1-13; 2 Samuel 7:8-16 (summary only)
**Story:** The prophet Samuel was sent to Bethlehem to choose a new king. Jesse paraded seven fine, tall sons. None was the one. "Are all your children here?" Samuel asked. "There remains yet the youngest. Behold, he is keeping the sheep." They sent for David. God said, "Arise! Anoint him, for this is he." God doesn't choose by height or looks; God looks at the heart. Later God made David a great promise: one of his family would reign for ever. Bethlehem again, sheep again. Hold on to those two words. They will come back on Christmas night.
**Wonder:** I wonder why God picks the one nobody expected?
**Activity:** Draw someone in your life who is "overlooked" and say thank you to them.
**Prayer:** God who sees the heart, help us to value the people others overlook, including ourselves. Amen.
**Illustration:** A boy with a staff and a sheep on a hillside; a horn of oil shown as a gold curl above him; thread spirals up from him to a crown shape in the sky. Green and gold.
**Alt-text:** A shepherd boy with a staff and sheep on a hill, with a golden crown-shaped thread rising above.
**Hidden detail:** A tiny harp leaning on a rock.
**Local link:** Hill sheep country around Kington and Radnor Forest. General only; *needs confirmation by the parish* for any named farmer or flock.

### Day 11: "The People in the Dark"
**Bible:** Isaiah 9:2, 6-7 (KJV quote: "The people that walked in darkness have seen a great light")
**Story:** Hundreds of years went by. Kings came and went; many were unkind or foolish. The people were frightened of enemies and tired of broken promises. Into that dark, the prophet Isaiah spoke bold words: "The people that walked in darkness have seen a great light" (KJV). He saw a child coming, a child who would be a wonderful counsellor, a mighty God, an everlasting father, a prince of peace. Isaiah did not know the day, or the name, or the town. But he held up the promise like a lamp: *light is coming.* Many of us hear that in our favourite carols.
**Wonder:** I wonder what light means to someone who has been in the dark for a long time?
**Activity:** Make a paper lantern, or turn all the lights off and then on, saying "Light is coming."
**Prayer:** Light of the world, shine on all who feel in the dark today, and on us. Amen.
**Illustration:** A crowd of small silhouettes in the dark; a large golden lamp-shape glows above them; the thread is a beam falling on faces (no features). Deep navy, bright gold.
**Alt-text:** A crowd of silhouettes in darkness looks up at a great golden light shining down.
**Hidden detail:** A candle held by a child.
**Local link:** Candlelit carol service. *Needs confirmation by the parish* before listing dates or churches; link out to the Services page.

### Day 12: "A Shoot from the Stump"
**Bible:** Isaiah 7:14; 11:1-9 (KJV quote: "a rod out of the stem of Jesse" Isa 11:1, or WEB wording)
**Story:** Isaiah also saw something sad: the royal family tree cut down to a stump, like a tree after a storm. Yet he promised that from the stump a new green shoot would grow. A child of Jesse's family, wise and fair, who would care for the poor. And a peace so deep that wolves would lie down with lambs and a little child would lead them. He also spoke of a baby with a name that means God with us: Immanuel. Look at an old tree stump in the woods: sometimes a green shoot does appear. God's hope often arrives small and green, in the places we thought were finished.
**Wonder:** I wonder what a world where wolves and lambs are friends would be like?
**Activity:** Plant a seed or a bulb (or draw a stump with a green shoot) and watch for growth.
**Prayer:** God of new beginnings, bring green shoots to the places in our lives that feel cut down. Amen.
**Illustration:** A cut tree stump with a bright green shoot; at the side a wolf lying beside a lamb, a child between. The gold thread wraps the shoot. Browns, fresh green, gold.
**Alt-text:** A green shoot grows from a tree stump, with a wolf and a lamb resting peacefully beside it.
**Hidden detail:** A tiny bird nesting in the shoot.
**Local link:** Herefordshire woodland and old orchards. Natural, general image. *Needs confirmation by the parish* for any named wood or orchard.

### Day 13: "Small Town, Big Promise"
**Bible:** Micah 5:2-5 (KJV quote: "But thou, Bethlehem Ephratah, though thou be little...")
**Story:** Micah was a country prophet who spoke up for ordinary people. He did not name a great city. He named a tiny one: "But thou, Bethlehem Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Judah, yet out of thee shall he come forth" (KJV). Bethlehem was a village. Nobody important lived there. Yet God said a ruler, one whose roots go back to long ago, would come out of it, and he would feed his flock and bring peace. So when we reach the stable, remember: this was foretold. Small places matter to God, and small people too.
**Wonder:** I wonder what it means that God's big plans often start in small places?
**Activity:** Make a "map of small things" showing places in your village you love.
**Prayer:** God of small places, bless our village and town, and show us how much they matter to you. Amen.
**Illustration:** A tiny cluster of houses on a hill, drawn small against a huge sky; the gold thread descends from the sky and pools on the smallest roof. Teal, cream, gold.
**Alt-text:** A tiny hill village under a huge sky with a golden thread reaching down to one small rooftop.
**Hidden detail:** A tiny sheep on the highest roof.
**Local link:** Kington as a small market town; Titley, Kinnerton and Huntington as small villages. Natural, no facts claimed.

### Day 14: "Songs by the River"
**Bible:** Psalm 137:1-4 (short, gentle excerpt); Jeremiah 29:4-14; Jeremiah 31:31-34
**Story:** Then came the worst thing of all. Enemy armies took the city, and many people were marched far away to Babylon. They sat by a river and could hardly sing. "How can we sing Yahweh's song in a foreign land?" they asked. They missed their home, their temple, their friends. But God sent a message through Jeremiah: settle down, plant gardens, and pray for the place you live in. God had not forgotten them. He would bring them back and give them hope and a future. And a new promise: a time when God's law would be written not on stone but on their hearts. Even in exile, God was planning a way home.
**Wonder:** I wonder what keeps hope alive when you are far from home?
**Activity:** Write a short letter or card to someone who is far away or lonely.
**Prayer:** God of the far-away and the homesick, hold those who are lost or lonely and bring them home. Amen.
**Illustration:** A broad river at dusk, with harps hung on willow branches; small figures sit on the bank; the gold thread crosses the river like a bridge to the far shore. Slate blue, willow green, gold.
**Alt-text:** Harps hang from willow trees beside a river at dusk, with a golden thread crossing to the far bank.
**Hidden detail:** A tiny house on the far shore with a light on.
**Local link:** None needed. Avoid implying anything about local people being "in exile".
**Review note:** Skip Psalm 137:7-9, which is a hard text. Keep to v.1-4.

### Day 15: "Comfort, and a Long Quiet"
**Bible:** Isaiah 40:1-5; Malachi 3:1; 4:2 (the "silent years" are a historical gap, not a biblical text)
**Story:** At last some of the people came home. The temple was rebuilt, though it wasn't as grand. Then came a long quiet. The prophets stopped speaking for a very long time, and the people waited. "Comfort, comfort my people," Isaiah had said, and spoke of a voice calling, "Prepare the way of Yahweh in the wilderness!" Malachi said a messenger would come, and that the sun of righteousness would rise "with healing in its wings" (WEB). Waiting is hard. Perhaps you are waiting for something now. But in the quiet, God was still at work, getting the world ready. The silence was not an ending. It was a held breath.
**Wonder:** I wonder what is easier to hear in a long quiet?
**Activity:** Sit in total silence for one minute, together, and then say what you noticed.
**Prayer:** God of patient love, help us to wait well, and trust that you are working even when we cannot see. Amen.
**Illustration:** An empty road across a hilly landscape stretching to a pale glow at the horizon; the gold thread is a faint line along the road. Soft greys and blues, a hint of gold at the edge. The calmest picture of the calendar.
**Alt-text:** An empty road crosses quiet hills towards a faint golden glow on the horizon.
**Hidden detail:** An hourglass (very small) beside the road.
**Local link:** A quiet churchyard in winter. *Needs confirmation by the parish* before naming a particular churchyard.
**Review note:** Do not assert "400 years of silence" as biblical fact; call it "a long quiet".

### Day 16: "An Old Man at the Altar"
**Bible:** Luke 1:5-25, 57-64 (summary)
**Story:** Zechariah was a priest, and he and his wife Elizabeth were both old, kind and faithful. They had always wished for a child, but it never happened. One day, as Zechariah served in the temple, an angel appeared. The angel told him not to be afraid: his prayer had been heard, Elizabeth would have a son, and he was to call him John, who would get people ready for the Lord. Zechariah could hardly believe it. "How can I be sure of this?" The angel said, "I am Gabriel, who stands in the presence of God." And because Zechariah doubted, he could not speak until the baby was born. Months later, when asked the baby's name, Zechariah wrote, "His name is John." Suddenly his voice came back, in praise.
**Wonder:** I wonder what Zechariah thought about in all those quiet months?
**Activity:** Try a "silent hour" or a no-talking game, then talk about how it felt.
**Prayer:** God who answers prayer in your own time, give us patience and faith. Amen.
**Illustration:** A candle-lit temple interior with an incense altar, an old man with raised hands, a tall, softly drawn angel shape; gold thread curls from the altar smoke. Warm reds, creams, gold.
**Alt-text:** An old priest at a temple altar sees a tall golden angel appearing in the rising incense smoke.
**Hidden detail:** A tiny scroll with the name "John" (or just a scribbled line).
**Local link:** None needed.
**Review note:** Don't present Zechariah's muteness as punishment in a harsh way; present it as a sign.

### Day 17: "Yes, Mary"
**Bible:** Luke 1:26-38 (KJV quote: "Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it unto me according to thy word")
**Story:** Mary was a young woman in Nazareth, an ordinary village. One day the angel Gabriel visited her. Gabriel greeted her and said that God was with her. Mary was puzzled and afraid. Gabriel told her not to be afraid: she would have a son, she was to name him Jesus, he would be great, and his kingdom would never end. Mary asked a sensible question: how could this be? Gabriel explained that this was God's own doing, and added that her cousin Elizabeth was also expecting. Then Mary said, "Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it unto me according to thy word" (KJV). She said yes. In that quiet yes the whole world turned.
**Wonder:** I wonder what it took for Mary to say yes?
**Activity:** Pick one small "yes" you'll offer someone today: a helping hand or an open ear.
**Prayer:** God, give us the courage of Mary to say yes to your call, even when it is hard. Amen.
**Illustration:** A simple room, a young woman seated, a tall angel shape (wings as gold thread) bending towards her; a lily in a jug; a window with a star. Blue (the traditional colour for Mary) and gold.
**Alt-text:** A young woman in a plain room listens to a tall winged angel whose wings are made of golden thread.
**Hidden detail:** A white lily in a jug.
**Local link:** Many churches have Marian imagery. *Needs confirmation by the parish* before naming a particular window or carving in any of the five churches.
**Review note:** Anglican readers vary on how to speak of Mary. Keep to the text ("favoured", "blessed") and avoid doctrinal claims beyond Luke.

### Day 18: "Joseph's Dream"
**Bible:** Matthew 1:18-25
**Story:** Joseph was a carpenter engaged to Mary. When he learned she was expecting a baby, he was heartbroken and puzzled. He was a gentle, good man, and he planned to quietly end the engagement so she would not be shamed. But in the night, in a dream, an angel told him not to be afraid to take Mary as his wife, because the child was from God. He was to call him Jesus, because he would save his people. Joseph woke up and did exactly that. He never says a word in the Gospels, but what he *does* speaks loudly. He stays, he protects, he trusts. The prophet's old word comes true: Immanuel, "God with us."
**Wonder:** I wonder what Joseph felt when he woke up from that dream?
**Activity:** Do a quiet act of kindness today without telling anyone.
**Prayer:** God, make us gentle and brave like Joseph, ready to protect and care for those who need us. Amen.
**Illustration:** A figure asleep on a simple bed, with a dream cloud above him showing a small star and a cradle, drawn in gold thread. Carpenter's tools on the floor. Warm brown, cream, gold.
**Alt-text:** A sleeping carpenter dreams of a small golden star and cradle drawn in a thread of light.
**Hidden detail:** A tiny saw or hammer in a corner.
**Local link:** Woodwork / carvings in churches. *Needs confirmation by the parish.*

### Day 19: "Two Cousins and a Song"
**Bible:** Luke 1:39-56 (KJV quote: "My soul doth magnify the Lord" v.46)
**Story:** Mary set off at once to visit Elizabeth, a long walk into the hill country. When Mary called out a greeting, the baby inside Elizabeth leapt for joy, and Elizabeth cried out that Mary was blessed among women, and so was the child she carried. Two women, one old and one young, both with astonishing news, held each other. Then Mary sang. "My soul doth magnify the Lord, and my spirit hath rejoiced in God my Saviour" (KJV). Her song is about a God who lifts up the humble, feeds the hungry, and turns the world the right way up. It's been sung in churches ever since, as the Magnificat.
**Wonder:** I wonder who you would run to if you had big news?
**Activity:** Ring or visit a friend or relative and share something good.
**Prayer:** God who lifts the lowly, fill us with joy and make us glad to share good news. Amen.
**Illustration:** Two women embracing at a doorway at the top of a hill path; the gold thread loops in a heart-shaped line around them; musical-note shapes rise into the sky. Rose, blue, gold.
**Alt-text:** Two women embrace at a doorway on a hilltop path, surrounded by a loop of golden thread and music notes.
**Hidden detail:** A tiny bird singing on the roof.
**Local link:** The Magnificat is sung or said in Evening Prayer. *Needs confirmation by the parish* before saying when or where it is used locally.

### Day 20: "The Long Road to Bethlehem"
**Bible:** Luke 2:1-5
**Story:** The emperor far away in Rome decided to count everyone in his empire, and everyone had to go to their family town to be counted. Joseph's family came from Bethlehem, David's town, so Joseph and Mary had to travel about eighty miles from Nazareth. On foot, or perhaps with a donkey, over rough hills, with Mary's baby due very soon. It is a long, tiring road. The emperor thought he was in charge, and was moving everyone like pieces on a board. But in the story, God was using even this to bring the promise to the right town at the right time. Micah's tiny Bethlehem was getting closer.
**Wonder:** I wonder what Mary and Joseph talked about on the long road?
**Activity:** Go for a walk together, counting steps or landmarks, and have a snack at the "halfway".
**Prayer:** God of every journey, bless all who are travelling today, especially those who travel with little. Amen.
**Illustration:** A figure with a donkey climbing a hill path; the road becomes the gold thread leading to a small town and a bright star above. Dusk colours, long shadows.
**Alt-text:** A couple and a donkey climb a hill road towards a small town under a star, the road shining like gold thread.
**Hidden detail:** A tiny milestone with a "B".
**Local link:** Local footpaths and hill roads. Walk-to-church ideas. *Needs confirmation by the parish* before suggesting any particular route or event.
**Review note:** The "eighty miles" is a common estimate (about 90 miles by road); say "about" or "many days' walk". The donkey is traditional, not in the text; mark as "perhaps".

### Day 21: "Travellers Following a Star"
**Bible:** Matthew 2:1-2; Isaiah 60:1-3 (WEB wording for "nations will come to your light")
**Story:** Far to the east, in a land beyond the desert, some wise people who studied the stars noticed a new one. They believed it meant a great king had been born. So they packed gifts and set out on a long journey with no certain map. We will meet them properly on 6 January, when they arrive. But their journey starts here, because the old promise had always said that people from far-off nations would come to the light. Isaiah wrote: "Nations will come to your light" (WEB). The Christmas story is for everyone, near and far, shepherd and scholar, village and city.
**Wonder:** I wonder what the travellers hoped to find at the end of the road?
**Activity:** Cut out a star and put it in a window that faces east, as a signal to travellers.
**Prayer:** God of all nations, welcome the stranger and the seeker, and bring all to your light. Amen.
**Illustration:** Three small figures on camels (or simply walking) far in the background, crossing dunes beneath a very bright large star; the gold thread is the star's tail running ahead of them. Deep purple, indigo, gold.
**Alt-text:** Distant travellers cross desert dunes beneath one bright star, its golden tail leading them on.
**Hidden detail:** A tiny crown and a tiny gift box on a camel.
**Local link:** None needed.
**Review note:** The Bible does not say there were three, or that they were kings; Matthew mentions three gifts. This door says "some wise people" and says plainly that they arrive later.

### Day 22: "Nowhere to Stay"
**Bible:** Luke 2:4-7, read only up to the words "no room" (the birth itself is held for Day 25)
**Story:** At last, Mary and Joseph reached Bethlehem. The little town was packed with visitors, all there to be counted. Joseph knocked on doors and found every place full. Mary was tired, and her baby was about to come. Luke tells us there was no room for them in the guest-place (the inn). Many scholars think it may have been the upper guest room of a family house, with animals sheltered on the ground floor, so the family may have ended up among the animals and their feed troughs. Wherever it was, it wasn't a palace. The King of kings came to the world with no place booked. That means he understands what it's like to be turned away.
**Wonder:** I wonder how it feels to knock and find no room?
**Activity:** Set an extra place at a meal or leave a candle in the window for someone who needs welcome.
**Prayer:** God, open our doors and our hearts to everyone who has nowhere to stay tonight. Amen.
**Illustration:** A street of closed doors with small yellow windows; Joseph and Mary at the last door; at the end a low stable with a soft glow; gold thread runs along the street to that glow. Night blue, warm lamp-yellow.
**Alt-text:** A couple stand at a row of closed doors at night, with a low stable glowing at the end of the street.
**Hidden detail:** A tiny cat on a doorstep.
**Local link:** Local support for people without housing, such as a foodbank or a collection. *Needs confirmation by the parish* before naming any charity or partner; check this fits their current appeals.
**Review note:** Gentle on the "innkeeper" tradition, which is not in the text. Luke 2:6-7 contains the birth itself, so this door cites 2:4-7 but stops reading at "no room"; the birth is held for Day 25.

### Day 23: "Shepherds in the Dark Fields"
**Bible:** Luke 2:8-12 (KJV quote: "Fear not")
**Story:** Outside the town, on the cold hillsides, shepherds were watching their sheep through the night. They were ordinary working people, up in the dark, doing a job that smelled of wool and wet grass. Suddenly the sky lit up and an angel stood before them. They were terrified. "Fear not," said the angel (KJV). He brought good news of great joy for everyone: that today in David's town a Saviour had been born, and they would find the baby wrapped in cloths and lying in a manger. The first people to hear were not kings or priests. They were the ones nobody asked. And God sent the news to them first.
**Wonder:** I wonder why God chose to tell the shepherds first?
**Activity:** Make a sheep from cotton wool, and add a small gold thread collar.
**Prayer:** God of the ordinary and the overlooked, thank you for sending good news to everyone. Amen.
**Illustration:** Dark hillside, three small shepherds with staffs, sheep curled up; a huge glow in the sky above with the thread bursting like a firework of light. Navy, gold, white.
**Alt-text:** Shepherds and sheep on a dark hillside look up at a burst of golden light in the night sky.
**Hidden detail:** A small lamb lifting its head to look at the light.
**Local link:** Hill farmers and lambing season. Natural for Herefordshire; a gentle thank-you to local farmers. *Needs confirmation by the parish* before naming any farm or event.

### Day 24: "Glory in the Sky"
**Bible:** Luke 2:13-16 (WEB: "Glory to God in the highest, on earth peace, good will toward men")
**Story:** And then it was not just one angel. The whole sky filled with them, a great choir praising God: "Glory to God in the highest, on earth peace, good will toward men" (WEB). The shepherds stood open-mouthed. When the angels had gone and the sky was dark again, they looked at each other. They said to one another that they should go to Bethlehem and see what had happened. They did not wait until morning or sort out the sheep. They hurried down the hill in the dark, towards the town, to find a baby in a manger. It's Christmas Eve. Tonight, perhaps, the hurry is in us too.
**Wonder:** I wonder what the angels' song sounded like?
**Activity:** Sing or hum a favourite carol together, as the shepherds might have, running down the hill.
**Prayer:** God of glory and peace, fill our hearts with your song, and send us to find you. Amen.
**Illustration:** A wide sky full of simple angel shapes made of gold thread curves, arcs and loops; below, the shepherds begin to run down a path towards a tiny glowing stable. Midnight blue, white, gold.

### Day 25: "Christmas Day: The Word Made Flesh"
**Bible:** Luke 2:6-7, 15-20; John 1:1-5, 14 (KJV quote: "And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us")
**Story:** And there, in Bethlehem, Mary's baby was born. She wrapped him in cloths and laid him in a manger, a feeding trough, because there was no other bed. The shepherds arrived, breathless, and found everything just as they had been told. They knelt, and then they went out singing, telling everyone what they had seen. Mary quietly treasured it all in her heart. John's Gospel tells it in the biggest words: in the beginning was the Word, and the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not put it out. "And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us" (KJV). The first light, the thread, the promise: here, a baby, God with us. Happy Christmas.

Warm gold, deep blue, cream. The brightest picture.

## 5. Content risks and review points for the vicar

1. **Doctrinal phrasing.** Days 17-18 (Mary's "yes", the nature of the conception) and Day 25 (John 1) should be read by the vicar. The text sticks closely to Luke, Matthew and John, but local emphasis matters.
2. **Tradition vs text.** Donkey, innkeeper, stable, three kings, "eighty miles", "400 years of silence". Each is either flagged as "perhaps/many think" or avoided. Confirm the parish is comfortable with that approach.
3. **Old Testament sensitivity.** Passover (Day 7) belongs to Judaism's living tradition: avoid supersessionist language ("replaced", "fulfilled and finished"). Prefer "echoes", "points towards". Consider a Jewish-Christian dialogue reading if the diocese offers guidance. Day 14: omit Psalm 137:7-9.
4. **Fall language (Day 2).** Gentle; avoid blaming Eve or implying children are "sinful".
5. **Violence and distress.** Last plague and the slaughter of Bethlehem's children are deliberately omitted for a young audience. Flag the omission to the vicar.
6. **Bible versions.** Only KJV and World English Bible are quoted (labelled in each day). The retellings are original paraphrase. Do not paste text from NIV, ESV, NRSV or Common Worship readings (copyright). Check the labels on the live site.
7. **Prayers.** All are original, short and non-liturgical. Do not describe them as authorised or Common Worship texts. Consider a one-line note: "These prayers are simple suggestions."
8. **Local facts.** Every local link above is optional and unconfirmed. Do not name any farm, service time, window, carving, charity, or historical detail unless the parish supplies and approves it. Service times must come from a single source of truth in the repo's config, not hand-typed into door text.
9. **Safeguarding and photos.** If visitors are invited to send photos (Day 3) or children's drawings, a parish safeguarding decision and consent process is needed first. Safer: leave user submissions out of v1.
10. **Artwork identity.** Faces left blank by design to avoid depicting Christ or specific ethnicities. Check this fits the parish's taste. Original artwork only: no stock "nativity" clip-art or trained-on-copyright imagery without licence.
11. **Magi timing.** Day 21 says they arrive later. Confirm the vicar is happy with a pre-Christmas "set out" door, since many nativity displays place them at the crib.
12. **Accessibility (for the build).** Alt-text above is provided for every picture; the hidden-detail hunt must not be the only way to enjoy a door. Door text should be real HTML, and reduced-motion respected for the thread animation. Dates: doors should only unlock on or after their day (decide whether previous doors stay open; sensible for late visitors).

## 6. Bonus and alternative doors

- **Bonus A, "Gifts for a King" (6 January, Epiphany): FINAL TEXT, approved by Greg and now in `modules/advent/content.mjs` (`bonus`, day 26).** Matthew 2:1-12 (World English Bible, quotations verified). The wise travellers (never "three": the Bible does not say) arrive, and the gold, frankincense and myrrh. Wording: "the wise travellers".
  - **Title:** Gifts for a King. **Reference:** Matthew 2:1-12.
  - **Paragraph 1:** On Day 21 some wise people set out from far away, following a star. Now their long road is nearly over. They reach Jerusalem, the great city, and ask everyone they meet, "Where is he who is born King of the Jews? For we saw his star in the east, and have come to worship him."
  - **Paragraph 2:** King Herod was frightened when he heard this, because he thought of himself as the only king. His advisers told him the old prophets had named the place: Bethlehem, the small town from Day 13. Herod sent the travellers on their way. "Go and search diligently for the young child. When you have found him, bring me word, so that I also may come and worship him." The travellers could not know what was in his heart.
  - **Paragraph 3:** They set off again, and there was the star, going ahead of them until it stopped over the place where the child was. "When they saw the star, they rejoiced with exceedingly great joy." They went into the house and found the child with Mary his mother, and they knelt down before him. "Opening their treasures, they offered to him gifts: gold, frankincense, and myrrh."
  - **Paragraph 4:** Down the years, people have found meaning in the gifts: gold for a king, sweet-smelling frankincense burned in worship, and myrrh, a precious perfume. The Bible does not say how many travellers there were, or that they were kings. It tells us only that wise people from a far country came to honour a child, just as the old promise said: the light would reach the nations.
  - **Paragraph 5:** Warned in a dream not to return to Herod, they "went back to their own country another way." Each year on 6 January the church keeps this story as Epiphany, when the light is shown to the whole world. The golden thread that began as a spark on the first morning has been God's gift to everyone all along. Happy Epiphany.
  - **Wonder:** I wonder what you would bring, if you could give the child a gift that was truly yours?
  - **Try this:** Choose or make a small gift for someone, and as you give it, tell them why you chose it.
  - **Prayer:** Wise and generous God, you lead people from far and near to the light. Receive our gifts, large and small, and guide us home by a new way. Amen.
  - **Alt-text:** The wise travellers kneel with gifts at a doorway beneath a bright star, a golden thread flowing from star to door.
  - **Hidden detail:** A tiny crown resting on the camel's blanket, plus the tiny star near the left horizon.
